Big words, made simple

Van power comes with a lot of jargon. Here's what each word actually means, in everyday English. You don't need to memorise any of it: come back whenever a word trips you up.

How power is measured

Volt (V)
How hard the power pushes. Your van runs on 12 volts; the plugs at home are 240 volts. Think of it like water pressure in a hose.
Amp (A)
How much power is flowing right now. A phone charger pulls a little; a kettle on an inverter pulls a lot. Like how much water is coming out of the hose.
Amp-hour (Ah)
How much power a battery holds, or how much you use in a day. A 100Ah battery holds twice as much as a 50Ah one. If your gear uses 40Ah a day, a 100Ah lithium battery lasts about two days on its own.
Watt (W)
Power at a glance: volts multiplied by amps. Solar panels and appliances are usually labelled in watts, like a 200W panel or a 60W laptop charger.
Watt-hour (Wh)
Like amp-hours, but in watts. A 100Ah battery at 12V holds about 1,200Wh. Handy when comparing portable power stations.
12V
The low, safe voltage your van, caravan or 4WD runs on. Fridges, lights, fans, pumps and USB chargers all run straight off 12V.
240V
Normal house power, from a wall plug. In a van it comes from an inverter or a powered campsite. Never work on 240V wiring yourself: that's a job for a licensed electrician.

Batteries

House battery (second or auxiliary battery)
The extra battery that runs your fridge, lights and gear, separate from the battery that starts the engine. So you can't flatten your way out of driving home.
Starting battery
The battery that starts your engine. You want to keep this one full, which is why the fridge runs off a separate house battery.
Dual battery setup
A starting battery plus a house battery, with a charger in between so driving fills both.
Lithium (LiFePO4)
The modern battery type for vans. You can use nearly all of it, it's light, and it lasts thousands of charges. Costs more up front.
AGM
The older, cheaper type of deep-cycle battery. You should only use about the top half of it, and it's roughly three to four times heavier than lithium for the same usable power.
Deep-cycle battery
A battery built to be run down and filled up again and again, unlike a car's starting battery, which only gives short bursts.
Usable capacity
How much of the battery you can actually use. About 90% of a lithium battery, but only about 50% of an AGM.
BMS (battery management system)
The little brain inside a lithium battery. It stops the battery getting too full, too empty, too hot or too cold.
Battery monitor (shunt)
Your fuel gauge for power. A small sensor on the battery cable counts every bit going in and out, so you see a real percentage instead of guessing.
Portable power station
A battery, charger and plugs in one box you carry around. Easy, no wiring, but usually smaller and pricier per Ah than a proper dual battery setup.

Charging

DC-DC charger
A smart charger that fills your house battery from the engine while you drive, without draining the battery that starts the van. Many also take solar.
Smart alternator
Most newer cars have one. It only charges when the car thinks it needs to, which is why a simple switch isn't enough and you need a DC-DC charger.
Alternator
The part of the engine that makes electricity while it runs, a bit like a bike light dynamo.
Solar regulator (charge controller)
The box between your solar panels and battery. It stops the battery overcharging and gets the most out of the sun.
MPPT
The smarter kind of solar regulator. It squeezes more charge out of the same panels, especially when it's cloudy or cold. Many DC-DC chargers have one built in.
PWM
The simpler, cheaper kind of solar regulator. Fine for small setups, but it wastes more of what your panels make.
Powered site
A campsite with a 240V plug. You can charge your battery and run house appliances from it.
240V battery charger
A charger that plugs into a wall or powered site to fill your house battery.

Solar

Fixed panel (roof panel)
A solar panel bolted to the roof. Set and forget, and it charges while you drive, but the van has to sit in the sun.
Solar blanket
A folding, portable panel you lay out in the sun. You can park in the shade and still charge. It packs away, but you set it up each time.
Peak sun hours
How many hours of full-strength sunshine a day is worth. A Queensland winter day gives about 4; a Tasmanian one, under 2. We use Bureau of Meteorology figures for every state.
Shading
Even a little shade across a panel can cut its output a lot. That's why blankets are handy when you're parked under trees.

Plugs, wires and safety

Inverter
Turns your 12V battery power into 240V house power, so you can use normal plugs. Handy, but it wastes about 15% along the way, so run things on 12V where you can.
Pure sine wave
The clean kind of house power that laptops, CPAP machines and coffee machines need. Cheaper "modified sine" inverters can damage sensitive gear.
Anderson plug
A chunky, standard connector for 12V gear in Australia. Grey or red, clicks together, handles big currents safely.
Fuse
A small safety part that breaks the circuit if something shorts or overheats, so a fault doesn't become a fire.
Circuit breaker
Like a fuse, but you can reset it with a switch instead of replacing it.
Isolator switch
One switch that cuts all power from the battery. Useful when working on the system or storing the van.
Cable size
How thick the wires are. Too thin and they heat up and waste power. Your auto electrician works this out for your setup.
Auto electrician
A licensed tradie who installs and checks 12V systems in vehicles. We always recommend having one wire in or check your setup.

Fridges

Compressor fridge
The common type of camping fridge (Dometic, Engel, Bushman and others). It works like the air conditioner at home: runs hard until it's cold, then switches off.
3-way fridge
A fridge, often in older caravans, that runs on gas, 240V or 12V. Great on gas, very hungry on 12V.
Duty cycle
How much of the time a fridge's motor is actually running. In hot weather it runs more, so it uses more power.
Low-voltage cut-out
A setting on your fridge that switches it off when the battery gets low, to protect the battery or make sure the car still starts.
