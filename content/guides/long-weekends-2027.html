2027 long weekends: how to turn annual leave into van trips

By Kiran and MitchUpdated November 20266 minute readHow we make money

The short version

  • In 2027, 11 days of annual leave can get you 28 days off, in every state and territory: 4 days around Australia Day, 4 after Easter and 3 between Christmas and New Year.
  • Every state then adds its own long weekends. The easy rule: when a public holiday falls on a Monday, take Tuesday to Friday off and you get 9 days for 4 days of leave.
  • Queensland and the Northern Territory get the best deal of the year in late April: Anzac Day and a May holiday are a week apart, so 4 days of leave gets 10 days off.

We plan our weekends away around public holidays, and 2027 is a good year for it. Christmas and Boxing Day fall on a weekend, so most of the country gets two extra public holidays on the Monday and Tuesday. Easter is early, at the end of March, when the weather is mild almost everywhere. Here are the best breaks for the whole country, then every state's extras, and a quick power note for each time of year.

These dates come from the Fair Work Ombudsman's 2027 public holiday list. Regional holidays (show days, local cup days) aren't all included, and your workplace may have its own rules, so check before you book anything.

Only want one day of leave? Take Monday 25 January and you get a 4-day Australia Day weekend (Sat 23 to Tue 26). Want a really long Easter? Take the week before as well (Mon 22 to Thu 25 March): 8 days of leave gets you 16 days off, from Sat 20 March to Sun 4 April.

In Tasmania, Easter Tuesday (30 March) is a holiday for the state public service only, so most Tasmanians still need the 4 days of leave.

A power note for each time of year

Where and when you go changes what your battery and solar have to do. A quick guide:

  • January and Christmas (summer): long days, so solar is at its best. But heat makes your fridge run much harder. Park the van in shade and put a portable panel or blanket out in the sun. Summer school holidays also mean busy campsites, so book early.
  • March and Easter (autumn): mild days and cool nights, easy on the fridge. Days are getting shorter after the March equinox, so a portable panel you can aim at the sun helps more than a flat roof panel.
  • June to August (winter): the shortest days of the year. In southern states a panel can make a quarter to a half of its summer output. Our winter solar guide has the numbers and fixes. Up north, winter is peak season and solar holds up well.
  • September to November (spring): days are getting longer and temperatures are kind. A good time to test a new setup before summer.
