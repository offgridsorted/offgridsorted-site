Toyota Hilux dual battery setup: what to know before you buy

By Kiran and MitchUpdated November 20267 minute readHow we make money

The short version

  • Diesel Hiluxes have an alternator (the part that makes power while you drive) that runs at a lower voltage than many others. REDARC says this kind can leave a second battery only 60 to 70% full through a simple isolator switch. So use a DC-DC charger, the charger that fills your second battery while you drive.
  • The 2015 to 2025 Hilux has room for a second battery under the bonnet, but the trays are small. Most lithium setups go in the tub, canopy or the rear of the cab, away from engine heat.
  • For a fridge-and-lights weekend, a 100 to 120Ah lithium battery with a 25A DC-DC charger is a common setup. Parts start from about $870, before fitting.

The Hilux is one of the most common utes on Aussie roads, so there's no shortage of dual battery kits for it. What makes the Hilux a bit different is how its alternator charges, how small the under-bonnet trays are, and the extra battery Toyota has tucked under the rear seat of some newer models. Here's what each of those means for you, and three setups at different budgets.

Which Hilux have you got?[[d:4wd:72]]

Battery trays and brackets are made to fit one shape of Hilux, so check your model before you buy anything.

  • 2005 to 2015 (the seventh generation, often called the N70). REDARC sells its charger bracket for this model as fitting March 2005 to September 2015.
  • 2015 to 2025 (the eighth generation, often called the N80). Most current kits are made for this one and are listed as "Hilux 2015 on". It's the one we focus on here.
  • Late 2025 on (the ninth generation). It went on sale in December 2025. Car reviewers report it sits on much the same chassis, cabin and tub as the model before, with the same 2.8 litre diesel. Even so, check that any kit you buy lists the new model before you assume it fits.

Your build date is on the plate in the driver's door jamb or in the owner's manual.

The Hilux alternator runs low

Most people have heard of smart alternators, the kind that turns charging down to save fuel. Toyota's diesels do something a little different. REDARC says Toyota has used temperature-compensating alternators on its common-rail diesels (sold as D-4D) since the early 2000s, and lists the Hilux among them. This kind of alternator lowers its charging voltage as things warm up under the bonnet. 4X4 Australia puts it simply: Toyota alternators run at a lower voltage than some others.

That's fine for the starter battery right next to it. Your second battery is further away, often at the back of the car, and it needs a bit more push to fill right up. Think of it like a garden hose on low pressure. It fills the bucket at your feet, but the one down the end of the yard only gets a dribble.

What this means for you:

  • Use a DC-DC charger, not a simple isolator. REDARC says these alternators may only charge a second battery to 60 to 70% at best through an isolator. A DC-DC charger lifts the voltage to what your battery needs, so it fills properly.
  • It matters even more for lithium. Lithium batteries want a higher, steadier charge than a low-voltage alternator gives on its own. A DC-DC charger with a lithium setting takes care of that.

Where the second battery can go[[d:battery:72]]

Under the bonnet (2015 to 2025). There's a spot in the engine bay for a second battery tray, and several brands make one.

  • ARB sells an engine-bay kit for the Hilux with a steel tray, wiring and a REDARC BCDC1225D charger (a 25A DC-DC charger). The battery isn't included.
  • The trays are small. 4X4 Australia compared the under-bonnet kits. Piranha's tray takes a battery up to 21kg, which it says isn't enough for a 100Ah AGM. Projecta's takes up to 17kg. So under the bonnet you're usually limited to a smaller battery.
  • Heat is the other catch. Both of those reviews note lithium options are limited under the bonnet. Most lithium batteries don't like engine bay heat. Our guide Can I put a lithium battery under the bonnet? explains why. If you do go under the bonnet, an AGM (a sealed lead-acid battery) and an engine-bay rated charger are the usual choice.

At the rear of the cab. ARB makes a Hilux 2015 on mount for its slimline lithium battery at the rear of the vehicle, so it doesn't eat into tub space. Space back here is tight in a Hilux. GoAuto notes the new model's rear backrest is still fixed, which limits fitting things like a slimline battery or an air compressor.

Check for a 48V Hilux first. Since 2024, some automatic double cab Hiluxes (SR, SR5 and Rogue) have V-Active, Toyota's 48-volt mild hybrid. Toyota says its 7.6kg lithium-ion battery sits under the rear seats and also feeds the car's 12V system. The ninth generation offers it on select grades too. If your Hilux has a "48V" badge, make sure any under-seat or rear-cab kit is listed for 48V models, and ask your auto electrician before you plan around that space.

In the tub or canopy. This is where most Hilux lithium setups end up. It's cooler than the engine bay, there's room for a bigger battery, and it's close to the fridge. In 4X4 Australia's 2026 Hilux build, the installer fitted two 100Ah lithium batteries in battery boxes beside the drawers, in space behind the wheel arches that would otherwise be wasted. The trade-off is a long cable run from the front. Electricity loses a bit of push over a long, thin cable (called voltage drop), so cable size matters. That's a job for your auto electrician.

Wherever it goes, the battery has to be held down in a proper tray or box. A loose battery in a sudden stop is a heavy, dangerous object.

Three Hilux setups at different budgets

A medium fridge (35 to 50L) with LED lights and phones uses about 33 to 36Ah a day in summer, by our estimate. A 100Ah lithium gives about 90Ah you can use, so that's roughly two and a half days with no charging. Every hour of driving with a 25A charger puts back roughly 20 to 25Ah.

What the extra money gets you. The budget Kings parts do the job, but Kings only gives a one-year warranty on the battery and doesn't publish the charger's warranty, so check with Adventure Kings. The mid-range iTechworld adds 20Ah, a Bluetooth app and a five-year warranty. The mid-range REDARC Core is a well-known brand but must go inside the car or canopy, not under the bonnet. The premium ARB kit costs about three times the budget parts. You're paying for a battery and mount made to fit the Hilux, a bigger 40A charger that fills the battery faster when you drive, and one company to call if something goes wrong.

Want more solar? A 200W solar blanket that you lay out in the sun while the Hilux sits in the shade adds roughly 58Ah on a good summer day, by our estimate. See Solar blanket vs fixed panel.

Watch the weight[[d:tent:72]]

A Hilux doesn't carry as much as you might think. 4X4 Australia lists a 2024 SR5 48V double cab with a 900kg payload (the most you can add, people included). A bullbar, canopy, drawers, fridge, water and two adults use that up fast. A 100Ah lithium battery weighs about 10 to 13kg. A 100Ah AGM is about 27 to 30kg, and you can only use about half of it. Size my Hilux setup
Get a pro to wire it in. A second battery means heavy cables, fuses and a connection to your Hilux's charging system, and some newer Hiluxes have a 48V system too. Have a licensed auto electrician install or check your setup, and never work on 240V wiring yourself.
