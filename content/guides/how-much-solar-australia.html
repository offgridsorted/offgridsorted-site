How much solar do I need? By state and season

By Kiran and MitchUpdated October 20266 minute readHow we make money

The short version

  • Work out how much power you use in a day, then divide by the sun hours where you'll be, then allow for real-world losses.
  • Southern winters need two to four times more solar than summers.
  • If the answer is more than fits on your roof, add a portable panel or plan to drive or plug in more.

"How much solar do I need?" is the question everyone asks, and the honest answer is: it depends on where you are and what time of year it is. The same panel that easily keeps up in a Queensland winter can fall well short in Tasmania. Here's how to work it out.

The idea: peak sun hours

A solar panel's watt rating is what it makes in perfect, full-strength sun. A day's sunshine is usually described in peak sun hours: how many hours of that full-strength sun the day is worth. A long summer day in Adelaide might be worth about 7 peak sun hours. A short, grey winter day in Hobart might be worth under 2.

The formula

Solar watts needed = daily use in watt-hours ÷ (peak sun hours × 0.7)

The 0.7 covers real life: panels lose output when they're hot, dusty, lying flat on a roof rather than angled at the sun, and a little more is lost in the regulator. Daily use in watt-hours is your daily amp-hours times 12. So 50Ah a day is 600Wh.

Peak sun hours are approximate long-term averages for each capital city, based on Bureau of Meteorology solar exposure data. Inland and northern areas are usually sunnier. In the Top End, the "summer" figure is the cloudy wet season. Use more for your own use, or less if you drive most days.

What the table tells you

  • Up north, solar is easy. Queensland and the NT need only modest panels all year.
  • Down south, winter is the problem. Victoria and Tasmania need around three to four times more solar in winter than summer.
  • Roof space runs out. Most vans fit about 300 to 600W of panels, caravans a bit more, and 4WDs less. If your winter number is bigger, you'll need another plan.

When the numbers don't fit

  • Add a portable solar blanket. You can point it straight at the sun and park in the shade, which makes it work harder than flat roof panels.
  • Lean on driving. A 25A DC-DC charger puts back about 20 to 25Ah per hour of driving.
  • Plug in now and then at a powered site, or carry a small inverter generator for long grey spells.
  • Use less. Your fridge is usually the biggest user. Keep it out of the sun, don't overfill it and open it less.

Tips to get more from your panels

  • Shade kills output. Even a shadow across part of a panel can cut its output a lot.
  • Angle beats flat in winter, when the sun is low.
  • Keep them clean, especially after dusty roads.
  • Heat costs power. Panels make less on scorching days, so allow extra in the outback in summer.

Size my solar
From Kiran and Mitch

In our Hiace we swapped a portable panel for a 160W Kings flexible panel fixed to the roof, running a Dometic CFF45 fridge off a Kings 115Ah AGM. On paper it looked close to enough. In real life, with the panel lying flat on the roof and an AGM we could only use about half of, it couldn't keep up when we stayed put. Adding a Kings solar blanket alongside it, through a splitter on the solar input, fixed it. More solar than you think you need is rarely wasted.
