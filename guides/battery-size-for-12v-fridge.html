What size battery do I need for a 12V fridge?

By Kiran and MitchUpdated November 20267 minute readHow we make money

The short version

  • For a typical 35 to 50L fridge with no charging, a 100Ah lithium battery gives you about three days in summer. A 100Ah AGM gives you closer to two.
  • Work it out as: fridge Ah a day x days without charging, then divide by how much of the battery you can use (about 90% for lithium, about 50% for AGM).
  • Don't run your fridge off the battery that starts your car. Use a second (auxiliary) battery, and add solar or a DC-DC charger so you aren't relying on the battery alone.

The fridge is usually the biggest power user in a van, caravan or 4WD, so it's the right place to start when you size a battery. Here's how to get from "what fridge do I have?" to "what battery do I need?", with the real numbers.

Step 1: how much your fridge uses in a day

Battery size is measured in amp-hours (Ah): how much charge it holds. A fridge's daily use is measured the same way, which keeps the maths simple. If your fridge uses 28Ah a day, it takes 28Ah out of the battery every 24 hours.

A 12V compressor fridge (the common type, like Dometic, Engel or Bushman) doesn't run all day. It switches on and off like the air conditioner at home, so its daily use depends mostly on how hot it is. These are the figures our Power Planner uses:

Estimates for a 12V system, a little above lab test figures to allow for lid opening and warm food. For where these come from and how heat changes them, see Will my fridge last the weekend?

If you know your exact fridge, check its spec sheet. Many list power use in Ah/h (amp-hours per hour). Multiply by 24 for a day. Remember lab tests keep the lid shut, so real life is usually a bit higher.

Step 2: how much of the battery you can actually use

The number on the label isn't what you get. Think of it like a water tank with a tap partway up: some of what's inside you can't, or shouldn't, drain.

  • AGM (a sealed type of lead-acid battery): use only about 50%. REDARC advises not discharging an AGM or lead-acid auxiliary battery below 50% charge, because going lower shortens its life. So a 100Ah AGM gives you about 50Ah.
  • Lithium (usually LiFePO4, lithium iron phosphate, the type sold for vans and 4WDs): use about 80 to 100%, depending on the brand. REDARC quotes up to 80 to 90% usable. Our planner counts 90%, so a 100Ah lithium gives you about 90Ah.

That's why a lithium battery does roughly the work of an AGM twice its size. More on that in Lithium vs AGM.

Step 3: how many days without charging

This is the bit only you can answer. How long might you go with no sun, no driving and no power point? A grey weekend parked under trees is the usual test. For most people, two days is a sensible target, and three if you stay put a lot or travel in winter.

Then put it together:

Battery size (Ah) = fridge Ah a day x days ÷ usable share

Example: a medium fridge in summer, two days, lithium. 28 x 2 = 56Ah. 56 ÷ 0.9 = about 62Ah. So a 100Ah lithium covers it with room to spare. The same job on AGM: 56 ÷ 0.5 = 112Ah, so you'd want a 120Ah AGM or bigger.

For comparison, REDARC's own fridge guide suggests about 60Ah over three days for a 35 to 52L fridge, and a 100Ah battery for it. Our figures sit higher because we size for hot Australian summers and a lid that gets opened.

Don't forget everything else

The table is for the fridge alone. Lights, phones, a fan, a laptop or a diesel heater all add to the daily total. Four LED lights in the evening, two phones and a 12V fan overnight add roughly 20Ah a day. With a medium fridge that takes you to about 48Ah a day, and two days becomes about 96Ah, just more than a 100Ah lithium gives you. The Power Planner adds it all up for you.

Dual-zone fridges use more

A dual-zone fridge has two compartments you can set to different temperatures, often one as a fridge and one as a freezer. Freezing takes a lot more power.

Dometic's figures for its CFX3 75DZ show it clearly. With both sides as a fridge (4°C, tested at 32°C outside), it's rated at 1.43Ah per hour, about 34Ah a day. With the small side as a freezer at -15°C, it's 3.04Ah per hour, about 73Ah a day. That's more than double, and it moves you up a whole row or two in the table. If you only need the freezer some of the time, run both sides as a fridge the rest of the time.

Why not just use the starter battery?

Your car already has a battery, so why buy another? Because it's built for a different job.

A starter battery (also called a cranking battery) is like a sprinter. 4X4 Australia explains it's designed to deliver a big burst of energy for only a few seconds, using thin lead plates. A fridge needs a marathon runner: a deep cycle battery, with thicker plates, designed to give out power slowly over a long time. 4X4 Australia notes a conventional starting battery can be damaged by being run down too far, too often, possibly in as few as five deep discharges.

The other problem is obvious: run the starter battery flat overnight and the car won't start. REDARC's advice is that running a fridge for more than a couple of hours after you stop driving needs a dual battery system. If you do run a fridge off the starter battery for a short drive, Dometic recommends setting the fridge's battery protection to High or Medium so it switches off before the car can't start.

A bigger battery isn't the only answer

A battery is your savings. Solar and driving are your income. If you top up most days, you can carry a smaller battery.

  • Driving: a 25A DC-DC charger puts back up to about 25Ah for each hour you drive. See DC-DC chargers explained.
  • Solar: enough panel to replace a day's use means the battery only has to cover nights and cloudy spells. See How much solar do I need?
  • Weight and space: lithium is much lighter than AGM for the same usable power, and needs about half the size. In a 4WD or small van, that matters.

If your fridge keeps switching off even with a decent battery, the problem is often charging, heat or cables, not battery size. See Why is my fridge flattening my battery?

Quick answers

What size battery do I need for a 35 to 50L fridge?

A medium fridge uses about 28Ah a day in summer. A 100Ah lithium battery runs it for about three days with no charging, and a 100Ah AGM for closer to two. These are estimates.

How much of an AGM or lithium battery can I use?

About 50% of an AGM, because running it lower shortens its life. About 80 to 100% of a lithium battery depending on the brand, and our planner counts 90%.

Can I run my fridge off my car's starter battery?

Not for long. A starter battery is built for a short burst to start the engine and can be damaged by being run down too often, and a flat one means the car won't start. Use a second, deep cycle battery.
