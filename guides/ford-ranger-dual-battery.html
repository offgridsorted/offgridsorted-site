Ford Ranger dual battery setup: PX and Next Gen

By Kiran and MitchUpdated November 20267 minute readHow we make money

The short version

  • Treat every Ranger from the PX onwards (2011 to now) as having a smart alternator, the kind that turns its charging down to save fuel. That means you want a DC-DC charger, the charger that fills your second battery while you drive, not a simple isolator switch.
  • Both the PX and the Next Gen (2022 on) have room under the bonnet for a second battery tray. The Next Gen Raptor doesn't, so its battery goes in the back or under a seat.
  • For a fridge-and-lights weekend, a 100 to 120Ah lithium battery in the tub, canopy or behind the rear seat with a 25A DC-DC charger is a common setup. Lithium usually stays out of the engine bay because of the heat.

The Ranger is one of the most popular 4WDs in Australia, so there are a lot of dual battery kits made for it. That's handy, but it also makes the choice confusing. Here's what's different about the Ranger, where the second battery can go, and three setups at different budgets.

Which Ranger have you got?

Kit makers split the Ranger into two families, and parts for one usually don't fit the other.

  • PX (2011 to 2022). This covers three updates: the PX1, PX2 and PX3. Most PX kits are listed as "Ranger PX" and fit all three, but check the listing.
  • Next Gen (2022 on). Sometimes called the RA. It shares a lot with the current Everest, so many Next Gen kits list both.

Your build date is on the plate inside the driver's door or in the owner's manual. If a kit says it suits "2011 on", it usually means the PX only.

The Ranger has a smart alternator

The alternator is the part of the engine that makes electricity while you drive. Older cars had a fixed one that charged at a steady voltage the whole time. Newer ones are smart (also called variable voltage). Once the starter battery is full, the car turns the charging right down to save a little fuel.

CarsGuide confirms that even a 2014 Ranger, an early PX, has a smart alternator. It also notes the system doesn't always recognise that a second battery has been added. Think of it like a tap the car turns down once its own glass is full. Your second glass, down the other end of the bench, barely gets a trickle.

What this means for you:

  • Use a DC-DC charger. REDARC says a smart alternator often won't charge a second battery to a usable level through a simple isolator. A DC-DC charger takes whatever the alternator gives and turns it into a proper charge.
  • Connect the ignition wire. On REDARC BCDC chargers, a blue wire goes to a source that's only live when the key is on. It tells the charger the engine is running even when the voltage is low.
  • No need to switch off the smart system. Some people have a dealer disable it. REDARC says a Ranger with Ford's Smart Charge doesn't need that, just a compatible DC-DC charger. Tinkering with the car's charging system can cause other problems.

More on this in Does my car have a smart alternator? and DC-DC chargers explained.

Where the second battery can go

Under the bonnet. Both Ranger families have a spot in the engine bay for a second battery tray.

  • PX: ARB sells a kit made for the Ranger PX with a 3mm steel tray, wiring and a REDARC BCDC1225D charger. The tray takes a battery up to 30kg. The battery isn't included.
  • Next Gen: Ford built in a space for a second battery when it designed this model. 4X4 Australia reports that the four-cylinder and V6 diesel models have room for it, and that a Ford-backed ARB kit includes the tray, a REDARC charger and wiring. Aftermarket trays fit the same space. The Pirate Camp Co tray, for example, takes a battery up to 25kg and 190mm tall.
  • Raptor: 4X4 Australia says the ARB kit doesn't suit the Next Gen Raptor, and reported ARB was working on a kit to put the battery under the rear seat or in the back instead.

The catch is heat. 4X4 Australia's Ranger guide puts it bluntly: don't put a lithium battery under the bonnet, find a spot in the tray or under a seat instead. REDARC says under-bonnet installs are less common now because of the lack of space and the heat. If you do go under the bonnet, a lead-acid type like AGM (a sealed lead-acid battery) is the usual choice, and only a charger rated for the engine bay. REDARC's BCDC Classic and Alpha can go there. The cheaper BCDC Core is for inside the car only. Our guide Can I put a lithium battery under the bonnet? goes through it.

Behind or under the rear seat. On dual cabs, some kits fit a slim battery behind or under the back seat. It's cool, secure and out of the weather, and it's a common choice for the Raptor. The trade-off is space: these batteries are often smaller.

In the tub or canopy. This is where most lithium setups end up. It's cooler than the engine bay, there's room for a bigger battery, and it's close to the fridge. The downside is a long cable run from the front. Electricity loses a bit of push over a long, thin cable (this is called voltage drop), so the cable size matters. That's a job for your auto electrician.

Wherever it goes, the battery has to be held down in a proper tray or box. A loose battery in a sudden stop is a heavy, dangerous object.

Three Ranger setups at different budgets

A medium fridge (35 to 50L) with LED lights and phones uses about 33 to 36Ah a day in summer, by our estimate. A 100Ah lithium gives about 90Ah you can use, so that's roughly two and a half days with no charging. Every hour of driving with a 25A charger puts back roughly 20 to 25Ah.

What the extra money gets you. The budget Kings parts do the job, but Kings only gives a one-year warranty on the battery and doesn't publish the charger's warranty, so check with Adventure Kings. You're paying for the brand's support, a charger that can live in the engine bay, and one company to call if something goes wrong.

Watch the weight

A Ranger loaded with a bullbar, canopy, drawers, fridge and water can get close to its GVM (gross vehicle mass, the most it's allowed to weigh fully loaded). A dual battery setup adds to that. A 100Ah lithium weighs about 10 to 13kg. A 100Ah AGM is about 27 to 30kg, and you can only use about half of it. Check the plate on your Ranger and our 4WD dual battery basics guide for more.

Quick answers

Does the Ford Ranger have a smart alternator?

Yes. CarsGuide confirms even a 2014 Ranger has one, so treat every PX and Next Gen as smart. Use a DC-DC charger with its ignition wire connected, not a simple isolator.

Where does the second battery go in a Ranger?

Both the PX and Next Gen have room under the bonnet for a tray, but lithium is better in the tub, canopy or behind the rear seat, away from engine heat. The Next Gen Raptor has no engine bay spot.

What size dual battery for a Ranger weekend?

For a medium fridge, lights and phones, a 100 to 120Ah lithium battery with a 25A DC-DC charger is a common setup. By our estimate that runs about two and a half days with no charging.
