The Weekender kit

Uses about 50Ah a dayHow we make money

Two or three nights away, a medium fridge, lights and phones. Drive there, set up camp, come home.

What it runs

  • Medium 35 to 50L fridge
  • 4 LED lights, about 4 hours a night
  • 2 phones charging
  • A 12V fan overnight
  • Water pump

What kind of solar?
Campervans: these kits are sized for you. The battery usually lives inside, so it stays cool, and the wiring run from the engine is short.
Caravans and camper trailers: fit the DC-DC charger in the van, close to the battery, fed by a heavy cable from the car through an Anderson plug. You'll get about a third less charge from driving than in a campervan because of the long cable, so lean on solar, and you'll usually have more roof space for it. A 240V battery charger for powered sites is worth adding (fitted by a licensed electrician). See Caravan power.
4WDs: Works well in a 4WD too. Use the solar blanket rather than roof panels, and if the battery goes under the bonnet, pick one rated for the heat. See 4WD dual battery basics.

Easiest way to build it

This size is perfect for an all-in-one power box: battery, DC-DC charger and solar input in one box you can move between cars. The box itself just straps in. The heavy lead that charges it from your engine battery should be run and fused by an auto electrician.

Travelling south in winter?

In southern winters the sun is weak, so the battery does most of the work over the weekend. In Victoria or Tasmania in winter, go for the 120Ah battery and the bigger 200W blanket.
